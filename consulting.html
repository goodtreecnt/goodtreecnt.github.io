Korea Field Research

We go there ourselves.

For Korean market research, desk research is only the start. Our team goes into the field in person — on foot, store by store and site by site — and reports what we actually see, not just what is published.

  • Stores & shelves — what is on display, where, at what price.
  • Clinics & healthcare sites — which products are actually used and how they are presented.
  • Distributors & manufacturers — visits to confirm that the company and its operations are real.
  • Trade shows & events — who is exhibiting, what is new, who is talking to whom.

Questions We Help Answer

Start with the decision you need to make.

Most research requests begin as a plain business question. These are the ones we hear most often.

“Who exactly is this company, and how established are they?”

Company profile, history, structure and publicly available background.

“Who else sells in this category, and through which channels?”

Competitors, distribution routes and how the market is organized.

“Which distributor or manufacturer would fit our product?”

A shortlist of candidates compared on fit, reach and decision speed.

“Is a comparable product already on the market?”

Similar products in your target market and how they are positioned and priced.

“What public regulatory information applies before we ship?”

Publicly available product and regulatory information, flagged for follow-up with specialists.

“Who should we speak to first, and how?”

Suggested next steps, plus communication and translation support where needed.

Sources & Method

Public information and on-site observation.

We tell you where each finding comes from — a public source, or something our team saw in person.

What we draw on

  • On-site field research — our team's own visits and observations in Korea.
  • Corporate disclosures — public filing systems such as DART in Korea and EDINET in Japan, where applicable.
  • Regulator information — publicly available product and regulatory data, for example from MFDS and PMDA.
  • Company & industry sources — websites, catalogs, press releases, trade publications and exhibitions.

What you can rely on

  • Sources are stated — public source or field observation, always labeled.
  • Unconfirmed items are flagged rather than presented as fact.
  • Local-language sources — we read Korean and Japanese sources directly; other markets are scoped with you.
  • Clear boundaries — we work from public information and our own observation, and do not claim access to confidential data.

FAQ

Common questions.

What does field research in Korea involve?

Our team visits the places that matter in person — retail stores, clinics, distributors, manufacturers and trade shows — checks products, prices, displays and operations, and reports back with notes and, where permitted, photos. We agree the places to visit and the questions to answer with you in advance.

Which markets do you cover?

Korea is our home base, where our team does field research in person. We also research Japan, and take on product and market research for markets around the world — scope and method are agreed with you before work starts.

What do you work from?

Publicly available information (corporate disclosures, regulator databases, company materials, press and industry sources) plus, in Korea, our own on-site observations. We state which is which in the deliverable.

Can you contact companies on our behalf?

Where the engagement includes connection, we can support first contact and communication between you and companies, suppliers or institutions — always as agreed with you in advance.

Is my inquiry kept confidential?

Client information is handled confidentially, and we are happy to agree to an NDA before you share sensitive details.

In what language will I receive the results?

In the language you need — English, Japanese or Korean — agreed at the start.

How long does a project take, and what does it cost?

It depends on the question and the scope. We agree the scope, deliverable and timeline with you before starting.
